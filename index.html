Foundational & Modern Sociology

The Thinkers' Gallery

Click any sociologist card to open comprehensive study notes, key terms, and exam application pointers.

Classical Theorist France (1858–1917)

Émile Durkheim

Pioneer of modern social science. Focused on social integration, collective consciousness, social facts, and religion as social glue.

Key Concepts: • Social Facts • Suicide Typology • Sacred & Profane • Mechanical & Organic Solidarity
Conflict Theorist Germany (1818–1883)

Karl Marx

Examined society through the lens of economic production, class struggle, historical materialism, and capitalist alienation.

Key Concepts: • Historical Materialism • Mode of Production • Alienation • False Consciousness
Interpretive Sociology Germany (1864–1920)

Max Weber

Introduced Verstehen (interpretive understanding), ideal types, rationalization, bureaucracy, and the link between religion and capitalism.

Key Concepts: • Social Action • Authority Types • Bureaucracy • Protestant Ethic
Structural Anthropology UK (1881–1955)

A.R. Radcliffe-Brown

Developed structural-functionalism by comparing social structures to biological organisms maintaining social equilibrium.

Key Concepts: • Social Structure • Function & Process • Andaman Islanders • Totemism
Ethnographic Method Poland/UK (1884–1942)

Bronislaw Malinowski

Championed intensive fieldwork and participant observation. Argued that cultural institutions satisfy basic psychological needs.

Key Concepts: • Participant Observation • Needs Functionalism • Kula Ring • Magic & Science
Indian Sociology India (1916–1999)

M.N. Srinivas

Titan of Indian sociology. Grounded sociology in rigorous village studies and introduced vital concepts regarding social mobility in India.

Key Concepts: • Sanskritisation • Dominant Caste • Westernisation • The Remembered Village

UPSC Optional (25 Marks) Paper 1 • Sociological Thinkers & Religion

"Discuss the relationship between religion and science in modern society. Do they necessarily remain in conflict?"

Analyze the transformation of religious institutions vis-à-vis scientific rationalization, drawing upon Durkheimian sacred/profane and Weberian disenchantment.

UGC NET (Objective & Descriptive) Indian Society • Social Stratification

Critically evaluate M.N. Srinivas's concept of 'Sanskritisation' in understanding contemporary social mobility in rural and urban India.

Examine how lower castes emulate upper caste lifestyle customs and the limitations of Sanskritisation in capturing structural economic mobility.

UPSC Optional (15 Marks) Paper 1 • Karl Marx & Work

Explain Karl Marx's four dimensions of alienation under the capitalist mode of production. Is alienation relevant in the modern gig economy?

Break down alienation from product, process, species-being, and fellow workers with modern examples of gig platforms and algorithms.

High-Scoring Framework

Student-Friendly Answer Writing Masterclass

Master the foolproof 3-step formula to transform average answers into top-tier examiner favorites.

1

Understand

Deconstruct command words (*Critically examine*, *Discuss*, *Comment*). Identify the core sociological sub-branch and thinkers implied in the prompt.

2

Grasp

Integrate classical & modern thinkers, empirical case studies, and sociological terms (e.g., *Anomie*, *Habitus*, *Verstehen*) to substantiate arguments.

3

Write

Structure neatly into Introduction (definition/quote), Body (balanced arguments with thinkers), and a forward-looking Sociological Conclusion.

Classical Sociologist (France)

Émile Durkheim (1858–1917)

As one of the principal architects of modern social science, Durkheim established sociology as an independent academic discipline. His work focuses heavily on how social order is maintained in modern societies.

1. Social Facts (*Les Faits Sociaux*)

External to the individual and endowed with coercive power. Divided into material and non-material social facts.

2. Division of Labour

Contrasts Mechanical Solidarity (sameness, repressive law) with Organic Solidarity (interdependence, restitutive law).

3. Theory of Suicide

Categorized suicide into Egoistic, Altruistic, Anomic, and Fatalistic based on integration and regulation.

4. Sociology of Religion

Formulated the dichotomy of Sacred vs. Profane; religion is society worshipping itself symbolically.

5. Anomie

A state of normlessness or moral deregulation occurring during rapid societal shifts.

6. Sociology of Deviance

Crime is normal and necessary as it reinforces collective moral boundaries.

Conflict Theorist (Germany)

Karl Marx (1818–1883)

German philosopher and economist whose critical theories revolutionized social stratification and political economy through class struggle.

1. Historical Materialism

Economic production forms the foundation of human history, evolving through successive socio-economic stages.

2. Base and Superstructure

The economic Base dictates the legal, political, and cultural Superstructure of society.

3. Class Struggle

Polarization between the Bourgeoisie (owners) and the Proletariat (wage labourers).

4. Theory of Alienation

Workers feel estranged from their product, production process, species-essence, and peers.

5. Surplus Value

Capitalist profit is derived from paying workers less than the actual value generated by their labour.

6. Class Consciousness

Transition from a class-in-itself to a class-for-itself, sparking revolutionary social change.

Interpretive Sociologist (Germany)

Max Weber (1864–1920)

Pioneer of interpretive (Verstehende) sociology who emphasized subjective meaning, cultural values, and administrative rationalisation.

1. Social Action

Action imbued with subjective meaning: instrumentally rational, value-rational, affective, and traditional.

2. Ideal Types

Methodological mental constructs used as measuring rods to analyze empirical social phenomena.

3. Types of Authority

Legitimized power categorized into Traditional, Charismatic, and Legal-Rational authority.

4. Bureaucracy

Hierarchical legal-rational administration leading to the "iron cage" of modern rationalization.

5. Protestant Ethic

Ascetic Protestantism psychologically fostered the rise of modern capitalist enterprise spirit.

6. Class, Status, Party

Three-component theory of stratification spanning economic, social prestige, and political dimensions.

Indian Sociologist (India)

M. N. Srinivas (1916–1999)

Pioneer of empirical, field-based sociology in India who transformed the study of Indian society from scriptural texts to living village ethnography.

1. Sanskritisation

Process by which lower castes imitate upper-caste lifestyles and rituals for upward social mobility.

2. Dominant Caste

Castes wielding numerical strength, land ownership, political clout, and ritual status locally.

3. Westernisation

Societal changes brought by British rule—technology, secular values, and legal systems.

4. Field-View vs Book-View

Advocated village immersion over relying solely on classical religious texts.

5. Secularisation

Reduction of sacred ritual influence in public, political, and everyday affairs in India.

6. The Remembered Village

Classic ethnographic monograph documenting rural social transformation in Rampura.

Structural Functionalist (USA)

Talcott Parsons (1902–1979)

Dominant American theorist who formulated grand structural functionalism, synthesizing classical European sociology into a unified action system.

1. Action Frame of Reference

Social action involves actors pursuing goals within norm-governed situations.

2. AGIL Schema

Four functional prerequisites: Adaptation, Goal Attainment, Integration, and Latency.

3. Pattern Variables

Five dichotomous choices defining role relationships in social systems.

4. Value Consensus

Social order is maintained when individuals internalize shared cultural norms.

5. Evolutionary Universals

Complex organizational traits like money, bureaucracy, and legal systems enhancing societal survival.

6. Sick Role Theory

Analyzed illness as an institutionalized deviant role with specific rights and obligations.

Functionalist / Middle-Range (USA)

Robert K. Merton (1910–2003)

Refined functional analysis by introducing middle-range theories, bridging abstract grand theory with empirical investigation.

1. Middle-Range Theory

Theories intermediate to general systems and rigorous empirical data collection.

2. Manifest & Latent Functions

Distinction between intended/recognized consequences versus hidden, unintended ones.

3. Strain Theory of Deviance

Deviance arising from disconnect between cultural goals and institutionalized means.

4. Reference Groups

Groups against which individuals evaluate their own status and achievements.

5. Bureaucratic Personality

Rigid adherence to rules leading to goal displacement in bureaucratic organizations.

6. Self-Fulfilling Prophecy

A false definition of a situation that evokes behavior making the original assumption come true.

Social Prism is an educational initiative dedicated to making Sociology accessible, engaging, and meaningful for students and aspiring civil servants. Founded by Pathan Musheer Khan, M.A. in Sociology from Savitribai Phule Pune University (SPPU), Pune, Social Prism aims to bridge the gap between sociological understanding and its practical application in academics and competitive examinations.

We believe that Sociology is more than the study of society — it is a way of understanding human behaviour, social institutions, inequalities, cultural transformations, and the forces that shape our everyday lives.

Our platform seeks to simplify complex sociological theories, explain the contributions of eminent thinkers, connect contemporary social issues with sociological perspectives, and help learners develop analytical thinking and structured answer-writing skills.

What We Offer

  • Core Concepts & Thinkers: Simplified explanations of sociological theories, concepts, and classical and contemporary thinkers.
  • PYQ Vault: Previous-year question-oriented learning to identify recurring themes and understand examination demands.
  • Socio-Lens: A sociological perspective on contemporary events, social issues, and transformations in Indian society.
  • Answer Writing Hub: Guidance on structuring analytical, evidence-based, and exam-oriented answers.
  • AI Answer Studio: An AI-assisted learning space designed to support answer practice, conceptual clarity, and revision.

Our Vision

Our vision is to create a learning space where students do not merely memorise sociological concepts but learn to question, analyse, interpret, and understand society through a sociological lens.

We aspire to make quality Sociology learning more accessible to aspirants preparing for UPSC Civil Services, UGC-NET, and other academic and competitive examinations.

Our Philosophy

At Social Prism, we believe that every social phenomenon has multiple dimensions, and understanding those dimensions is the first step towards meaningful learning.

We don't just help you study Sociology; we encourage you to think sociologically.

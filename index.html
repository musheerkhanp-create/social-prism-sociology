SOCIOLOGY THINKERS

Core Concepts

Thinkers, theories and exam-focused applications.

Émile Durkheim (1858–1917)

Functionalism, social order and collective integration.

Social Facts

External, general and coercive ways of acting, thinking and feeling.

Division of Labour

Mechanical solidarity gives way to organic solidarity through interdependence.

Suicide

Egoistic, altruistic, anomic and fatalistic types based on integration and regulation.

Religion

Sacred and profane; rituals reinforce collective consciousness.

Anomie

Weak or disrupted norms during rapid social change.

Method

Treat social facts as things; compare social rates and patterns.

Karl Marx (1818–1883)

Historical materialism, class conflict and capitalist exploitation.

Historical Materialism

Material conditions and production relations shape historical change.

Base and Superstructure

Economic relations interact with political, legal and ideological institutions.

Alienation

Estrangement from product, labour process, species-being and fellow workers.

Class Struggle

Conflict between groups with opposed relations to production.

Surplus Value

Difference between value produced by labour and value returned as wages.

Contemporary Link

Gig work, labour precarity and ownership of digital platforms.

Max Weber (1864–1920)

Social Action

Instrumentally rational, value-rational, affective and traditional.

Verstehen

Interpretive understanding of subjective meanings.

Authority

Traditional, charismatic and legal-rational.

Bureaucracy

Hierarchy, formal rules, specialised roles and impersonality.

Stratification

Class, status and party as distinct dimensions.

Ideal Type

Analytical construct used to compare empirical realities.

M. N. Srinivas, Parsons and Merton

Sanskritisation

Cultural emulation as a form of status mobility.

Dominant Caste

Local dominance linked to land, numbers and political power.

Westernisation

Institutional and cultural changes associated with Western influence.

Parsons: AGIL

Adaptation, Goal attainment, Integration and Latency.

Merton

Manifest and latent functions, dysfunctions and middle-range theories.

Strain Theory

Conformity, innovation, ritualism, retreatism and rebellion.

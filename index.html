Explore Sociology: Comprehensive Thinker Breakdowns

Complete theoretical breakdowns for UPSC Optional & UGC NET Sociology.

1. Émile Durkheim (1858–1917) — Classical Functionalist

Established sociology as an autonomous science studying social order and collective integration.

Social Facts: Ways of acting, thinking, and feeling external to the individual and endowed with coercive power (Material & Non-material).
Division of Labour: Shift from Mechanical Solidarity (similarity, repressive law) to Organic Solidarity (interdependence, restitutive law).
Theory of Suicide: Based on Integration and Regulation — Egoistic, Altruistic, Anomic, and Fatalistic suicide.
Sociology of Religion: Sacred vs. Profane dichotomy; Australian Arunta totemism; religion as society worshipping itself.
Anomie: State of normlessness and moral deregulation during rapid economic transitions.
Normalcy of Crime: Deviance is functional as it clarifies moral boundaries and enables social change.

2. Karl Marx (1818–1883) — Conflict & Materialist Theorist

Analyzed capitalism, class conflict, and historical change driven by material production.

Historical Materialism: Society evolves through modes of production: Primitive Communism, Slavery, Feudalism, Capitalism, Socialism.
Base & Superstructure: Economic relations of production (Base) shape law, politics, religion, and ideology (Superstructure).
Alienation (4 Types): Estrangement from the product, production process, species-being (creative essence), and fellow workers.
Class Struggle: Polarization between Bourgeoisie (owners) and Proletariat (wage workers).
Surplus Value: Unpaid labour value extracted by capitalists as the root of economic exploitation.
Class Consciousness: Transition from 'Class-in-itself' to 'Class-for-itself' leading to revolution.

3. Max Weber (1864–1920) — Interpretive Sociology

Pioneered Verstehen (interpretive understanding) of subjective meanings attached to human actions.

Social Action (4 Types): Instrumentally Rational (Zweckrational), Value-Rational (Wertrational), Affective, and Traditional.
Ideal Types: Conceptual analytical yardsticks used to compare empirical realities.
Authority (3 Types): Traditional, Charismatic, and Legal-Rational authority.
Bureaucracy: Rational-legal hierarchy, written rules, impersonality, and the 'Iron Cage of Rationality'.
Protestant Ethic: Calvinist doctrine of predestination fostered the ascetic spirit of modern capitalism.
Class, Status & Party: Multidimensional stratification based on economy (Class), prestige (Status), and power (Party).

4. M. N. Srinivas (1916–1999) — Architect of Indian Sociology

Shifted Indian sociology from textual Indology (Book-view) to empirical village ethnography (Field-view).

Sanskritisation: Lower castes adopting rituals, customs, and vegetarianism of upper castes for positional status mobility.
Dominant Caste: Castes holding numerical strength, arable land ownership, political power, and local ritual rank.
Westernisation: Institutional, ideological, and technological changes introduced through British rule.
Secularisation: Decline of ritual pollution rules and sacred influence in public spheres.
Field-View vs Book-View: Direct participant observation in villages (Rampura study) over scriptural generalizations.
Vote Bank Politics: Coined the term explaining how patron-client caste loyalties shape electoral mobilization.

5. Talcott Parsons & Robert K. Merton — American Functionalism

Talcott Parsons: Formulated the AGIL Framework (Adaptation, Goal Attainment, Integration, Latency), Pattern Variables (5 dichotomies of action), and Social System equilibrium.
Robert K. Merton: Introduced Middle-Range Theories, Manifest vs. Latent Functions, Dysfunctions, Reference Group Theory, and Strain Theory of Deviance (Conformity, Innovation, Ritualism, Retreatism, Rebellion).

Socio-lens Monthly & Current Affairs

Decoding contemporary Indian and global events through sociological frameworks.

Consumer Culture

The iPhone & Conspicuous Consumption: Veblen in the 21st Century

Thorstein Veblen’s Theory of the Leisure Class (1899) introduced Conspicuous Consumption—buying luxury goods to publicly display social status. In modern India, EMI-driven luxury purchases and social media flexing reflect Pierre Bourdieu’s concept of symbolic capital and status distinction.

Gender & Economy

The Informal Economy & Women: Dual Burden and Invisible Care Work

Over 85% of working women in India are concentrated in the informal sector without social security. Combining Ann Oakley’s Dual Burden and Sylvia Walby’s Private vs. Public Patriarchy helps explain how unpaid domestic reproduction subsidizes modern capitalism.

Digital Sociology

Algorithmic Tribes: Social Media & New Collective Effervescence

Durkheim spoke of physical gatherings creating Collective Effervescence. Today, recommendation algorithms manufacture digital echo chambers, viral moral panics, and virtual totems—polarizing public spheres (Habermas) while forging new online identities.

Answer Writing Hub

Follow the 3-Step Formula to score 280+ in UPSC Sociology Optional.

01. Understand

Deconstruct Directive Words

Identify whether the question asks to Critically Examine, Discuss, Evaluate, or Comment. Pinpoint the exact syllabus sub-topic.

02. Grasp

Integrate Thinkers & Empirical Studies

Never write a General Studies style answer. Substantiate every paragraph with sociological jargon, classical thinkers, and Indian village/urban studies.

03. Write

Intro • Multi-Dimensional Body • Conclusion

Start with a crisp sociological definition, present Functionalist, Conflict, and Feminist views in the body, and conclude with contemporary relevance.

Student Corner

Essential study strategies, revision techniques, and glossary checklists.

📌 Active Revision Strategy

Prepare 1-page thinker sheets containing: (1) Key Books with years, (2) Core Concepts, (3) 3 Major Criticisms, and (4) Contemporary Indian application (e.g., applying Weber's charisma to Indian electoral politics).

📌 Paper 1 & Paper 2 Integration

Always link Paper 1 Western theories with Paper 2 Indian thinkers. For example, when asked about Karl Marx's mode of production, cite A.R. Desai's Marxist analysis of Indian nationalism and Daniel Thorner's agrarian class structure.
